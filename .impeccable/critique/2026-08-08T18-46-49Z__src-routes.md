## Anti-Patterns Verdict

**Does this look AI-generated? Mostly no — and the parts that do are the parts nobody touched.**

**LLM assessment.** The designed surfaces have a real point of view. The timer bar converges on the shape every tracker uses and *says so in a comment* rather than inventing an affordance. `TotalsRow` explicitly refuses to become four bordered stat cards (`totals-row.tsx:5-11`). No gradient, no glass, no purple, no hero-metric grid, no uppercase eyebrows above sections. The empty state teaches rather than apologises. Sentence case throughout. That is not what generated UI looks like.

Four things a Linear/Stripe-fluent user would pause at:

1. **The sidebar was never retuned.** `sidebarMenuButtonVariants` ships `rounded-xl` (`sidebar.tsx:484`, ~0.63rem) while every content button is `rounded-md` (~0.36rem, `button.tsx:8`, deliberately, with a comment rejecting base-luma's radius). Tooltip `rounded-xl`, Dialog `sm:rounded-xl`, Popover `rounded-lg`, Skeleton `rounded-2xl`. **Six radii in one product**, and the two most co-visible ones disagree. This is verbatim the product register's banned "inconsistent component vocabulary."
2. **Full width + `justify-between`** is the single most common LLM layout default, and it is now the layout of every row, both remaining page headers, and every day header.
3. **A 1108px-wide text input** on `/reports` for typing a search term.
4. **`tracking-wide … uppercase`** shipped at `time-popover-fields.tsx:180`, in the most-opened popover in the app — the exact thing DESIGN.md's Sentence Case Rule names.

Verdict: **not slop, but not yet trustworthy at a glance on `/projects` and `/settings`.** The instrument is real; the chrome around it is stock.

**Deterministic scan.** `detect.mjs --json D:/work/trace/src` → `[]`, **exit 0, zero findings.** This was verified rather than taken at face value: `walkDir` discovered 95 scannable files (so the input was not empty), a synthetic control file containing `font-family:'Inter'`, `border-left: 4px solid #8b5cf6`, a glow and a gradient made the detector fire 2 findings and exit 2 (so the engine is live), and no ignore config was swallowing results. **However:** `detectText` is a regex engine over CSS-syntax declarations. This codebase expresses nearly everything as Tailwind utility classes, which the detector cannot see. A clean result means "no textual antipattern signatures", not "no antipatterns" — and the browser evidence found ten real defects the CLI structurally cannot detect.

**Visual overlays.** Not available. The Browser pane is not displayed, so the `detect.js` injection/overlay flow could not be presented and was not attempted. No user-visible overlay exists. Fallback signal is the measurement tables below.

## Overall Impression

This is a product with an unusually strong and *documented* point of view — the code comments record real design reasoning at the point of decision, which is rare and worth protecting. The core loop (start → run → stop → note) is better engineered than the category norm: never-blocked start, staged classification, a root-level undo architecture that survives navigation, and a hatch texture for absence that solves colour-blindness, forced-colors mode, and touch-target sizing in one stroke.

Three things undermine it, in order:

1. **A data-loss bug in the core value prop.** The note — the thing PRODUCT.md says *is* the product — is destroyed by Escape or a click-away, with no warning, no draft, no toast. It happens at the end of every session, to a user whose defining context is being interrupted.
2. **The full-width change is unspent.** The extra pixels went to gaps, not content. Measured: a 1050px-wide button rendering the string "V3". The single biggest opportunity is not to revert it — it is to *constrain the content instead of the page*.
3. **The un-domesticated shell.** The sidebar, tooltip, sheet, and skeleton are stock base-luma sitting inside a hand-tuned product, and the sidebar is the one element visible on 100% of screens 100% of the time.

## What's Working — do not "fix" these

**1. Never-blocked start with staged classification.** `timer-bar.tsx:158-189` holds project/tag/billable in local state *before a row exists* and hands them to the `start` mutation, and the button is never `disabled` (`:551`). There is no required field anywhere on that path. This is why capture costs nothing, which is the entire product thesis. Any future validation, required-project setting, or "are you sure?" here destroys the premise.

**2. The hatch as the empty-note affordance.** `entry-row.tsx:166-177` + `.hatch-empty` (`styles.css:166-180`) + `.touch-target` (`:257-268`) solves four problems with one texture: absence is visible without a colour; it survives forced-colors (the gradient drops, `border-color: currentColor` remains, `:175-180`); it reads as an invitation rather than a nag (no count, no red, no shame); and the `::after` hit-box buys WCAG 2.5.8's 24px without changing the 50px row rhythm — with the `truncate`-on-the-inner-span fix at `:143-153` keeping `overflow: hidden` from clipping it. Measured working on "Add a completed entry" (32px → 36px effective). Do not replace this with a badge or a count.

**3. The undo architecture.** `Toast.Provider` at root (`__root.tsx:97`) so undo outlives the surface that raised it; the snapshot captured *before* the mutation (`entry-log.tsx:63-92`); and the day-move undo carrying the original **instant** rather than re-deriving from a day string, so DST cannot land the entry an hour off (`:108-128`). This is the difference between a tracker you bill from and one you don't.

**4. Measured cleanliness that most apps do not have.** Across 8 page/viewport combinations: **zero text-contrast failures** (`--ink` #ece7dd on `--ground` #14110e ≈ 13:1; focus ring measured at **7.60:1**), zero console errors or warnings, zero failed requests, zero duplicate ids, zero unnamed buttons, zero images without alt, zero unlabeled form controls, zero prose blocks over 75ch, and no horizontal overflow at 375px. `.tabular` compliance is near-total. Don't let a polish pass regress any of this.

**5. `max-w-prose` as a *local* constraint.** The strategy documented at `app-shell.tsx:69-84` — "anything prose-shaped constrains itself locally rather than making every page pay for it" — is correct and is **working**: Settings hints measured at 534px = exactly 65ch on desktop, 41.8ch on mobile. The problem is that only the paragraphs adopted it; the containers around them didn't. Keep the strategy, extend it.

## Priority Issues — the 5 highest-value fixes, ordered

Ordered for the stated user: a freelancer with this pinned in a tab all day, looking at it in short bursts.

### 1. [P1] Escape or click-away silently destroys a typed note
**File:** `src/components/entries/note-sheet.tsx:95` (dismissal) · `:158-164` (Skip)

`onOpenChange` is wired straight through to `Dialog.Root` with no dirty check. Type three sentences, get pulled into a call, press Escape — the prose is gone. No warning, no draft, no toast.

**Why it matters for this user specifically.** "Short bursts, interrupted" *is* the persona. The note sheet opens the instant a timer stops, which is precisely the moment attention leaves. PRODUCT.md: "The note is the product… Any friction added to the note path is a direct attack on the core value" — and losing the note is worse than friction, it is the thing that makes a user stop trusting the tool. Peak-end bites hardest here because the note sheet *is* the end of every session. The sheet's own doc comment (`:22-25`) celebrates "three ways out, all of them cheap"; two of those three destroy user work.

**Fix.** If `value !== (entry.note ?? "")`, intercept dismissal: keep the draft in state keyed by `entry._id` so reopening restores it, and raise `Note not saved. [Undo]` using the existing toast-with-action pattern from `entry-log.tsx:76-88`. **Do not add a confirm dialog** — that adds the friction the sheet correctly refuses.
**Command:** `/impeccable harden`

### 2. [P1] `/reports` shows the Timer page's onboarding empty state while loading
**File:** `src/routes/_authed/reports.tsx:196` → `src/components/entries/day-list.tsx:36`

Verified in source. `reports.tsx:196` suppresses the *Reports* message during `LoadingFirstPage`, but the `else` branch still renders `<EntryLog groups={[]}>`, which falls through `DayList`'s early return at `day-list.tsx:36` to `EmptyLog` (`:103-141`). So Reports says: **"Nothing tracked yet. … How do the hours come back out? … Type what you're working on and press start."**

**Why it matters for this user specifically.** Reports is the invoice surface. The pinned-tab user glances at it and leaves — they see the flash, not the resolution. The moment of maximum anxiety in this product is "is my week actually in here?", and the app currently answers "nothing tracked yet" before answering correctly. It is the single most damaging sentence the product can say, in the one place it must never say it.

**Fix.** Give `DayList` an `empty?: ReactNode` prop so the owning page supplies its own empty state, and render the already-written-and-unused `Skeleton` (`ui/skeleton.tsx`) while `status === "LoadingFirstPage"` on both pages.
**Command:** `/impeccable harden`

### 3. [P1] Full width: constrain the content, not the page
**Files:** `src/components/shell/app-shell.tsx:85` · `src/routes/_authed/settings.tsx:48` · `src/routes/_authed/projects.tsx:30` · `src/routes/_authed/timer.tsx:103` · `src/components/history/filter-bar.tsx:103`

Measured at 1600×900: sidebar 256px, `main` 1344px, **no `max-width` on any of the four pages**. A `/projects` row is 1294px wide with a **1196px gap** between its leftmost and rightmost content, and the project-name button measures **1050px** to render the string "V3".

**Why it matters for this user specifically.** Glanceability is the whole job. Proximity is the strongest grouping cue available, and full width deletes it: a day label and its total, an entry title and its duration, a project name and its controls are *pairs*, and at 1600px+ they are unbound. The eye now has to travel 1200px in a 50px-tall row to answer "how long was that". That is not a short-burst glance; that is work. And on a wide monitor, a page holding six tracked hours now *looks* empty — a slow trust leak for a tool whose promise is "this will not lose your afternoon."

**Fix.** Not a revert. `max-w-[46rem]` on the `/settings` and `/projects` columns (one line each — they are forms, not tables). Cap the entry-row inner grid around 1100px while keeping the row background and bottom border full-bleed, so the log still reads edge-to-edge. Cap the `/reports` search input at `max-w-[24rem]` and drop its `flex-1`. Move `+ Add entry` next to the totals it relates to instead of the opposite corner. See the per-page verdict below.
**Command:** `/impeccable layout`

### 4. [P1] The app's primary input has a 0px focus indicator
**File:** `src/components/timer/timer-bar.tsx:494-495`

`#timer-title` carries `outline-none` and `focus-visible:ring-0`. Measured in the focused state with transitions killed: `outlineStyle: none`, `borderWidth: 0px`, ring box-shadow spread **`0px`**. The text caret is the only cue. There is no `:focus-within` treatment on the surrounding `<section>` either (grep confirms `focus-within` appears only in `entry-row.tsx:54`, `sidebar.tsx:575`, `projects.tsx:409`). Every other control in the app is fine — B measured 81/81 producing a visible change.

**Why it matters for this user specifically.** PRODUCT.md: "Keyboard before pointer. An all-day companion is used by muscle memory." This is the field that muscle memory targets twenty times a day, and it is the one control that gives no confirmation the keyboard arrived. It is also the *only* place Enter starts the timer (`:472-480`), so "am I focused here?" is load-bearing — and unanswerable.

**Fix.** Move the indicator to the container: `focus-within:border-ring focus-within:ring-3 focus-within:ring-ring/30` on the `<section>` at `:412-417`, gated so it doesn't fight `border-enlarger/50` while running. The bar is the control; the bare input inside it shouldn't need its own ring.
**Command:** `/impeccable harden`

### 5. [P1] 15 interactive controls are bordered at 1.37:1 — the app violates its own written rule
**Files:** `history/filter-bar.tsx:117, 132, 201, 262, 285` · `routes/_authed/settings.tsx:264` · (same pattern, behind dialogs, unmeasured) `manual-entry-dialog.tsx:230`, `note-sheet.tsx:140`, `time-popover-fields.tsx:173, 203`, `picker-list.tsx:142`, `projects.tsx:331`

Measured: `--edge-soft` #302d29 against `--ground` #14110e = **1.37:1**. `--edge` would give **3.15:1**. WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.11 requires 3:1 for non-text UI component boundaries, and PRODUCT.md states AA as the target. Failing controls at both 1600 and 375: both period arrows (30×30), all five filter chips (26px tall), both date inputs, the search box, the project select, and all three Settings selects.

This is not an oversight the design system missed — it is one it *pre-empted by name and by measurement*. `styles.css:29-34`: "`--edge` is the boundary of an interactive control (inputs, outline buttons) and must clear 3:1 against the ground per WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.11." DESIGN.md adds: "This value is set by WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.11, not by taste. **Do not darken it.**" The auth screens got it right via `ui/input.tsx:17` (`border-input`); every hand-rolled control in the app got it wrong.

**Why it matters for this user specifically.** Two visibly different input treatments in one product is exactly the "subtly-off component" the product slop test is about, and the filter bar is the surface this user opens when they are about to invoice. It also means the app reads as two products stitched together the moment you sign in.

**Fix.** `border-edge-soft` → `border-edge` on every input, select, textarea, chip, and icon-button. Keep `border-edge-soft` for `border-b`/`border-t` dividers and popover/dialog outlines only. Then extract one `fieldClass` constant — there are currently five near-identical local copies.
**Command:** `/impeccable audit`

**Immediate runner-up (#6):** the Cold Light Rule is being diluted in three places — see the violations table. It is cheap to fix and it protects the single most valuable signal in the product.

## Explicit verdict on the full-width change, per page

The wrapper is `app-shell.tsx:85` — `flex w-full flex-1 flex-col px-0 md:px-2`, no cap. Measured at 1600×900: sidebar 256px, `main` 1344px on three pages, 1329px on `/settings` (the only page tall enough to show a scrollbar — so **content jumps 15px between pages**, a small but real defect of its own). On a 1440px laptop with the rail expanded you get ~1168px, which is roughly the old `max-w-4xl` plus 270px and is genuinely fine. Damage begins around 1600 and is severe at 1920 and 2560.

### `/timer` — **serves it partially, then hurts. Cap the row, keep the log full-bleed.** *(source-derived; zero entries in the test account)*

The gain is real and the commit's own argument was right in principle: the note lives in a `truncate`d span inside the `flex min-w-0 flex-1` column at `entry-row.tsx:96`, so extra width does mean more note visible, and at 896px most notes truncated.

The cost is also real:
- Extra width beyond the title/note column becomes **empty gap** between that column and the `shrink-0` classifier cluster at `entry-row.tsx:193`. A 4-word title, then ~1000px of nothing, then a 250px control cluster, in a 50px-tall row.
- `timer.tsx:103` — `justify-between` puts the totals cluster hard left and `+ Add entry` at the far right. "I forgot to start the timer" is the highest-frequency recovery path in a tracker, and it is now orphaned in the opposite corner from the numbers that prompt it.
- `day-list.tsx:44` — day label and "3 of 5 noted" far left, the day total far right. This is the most-glanced pair on the page.
- A long note will run past DESIGN.md's own 65–75ch cap for note prose. (Unverified — no entries to measure.)

**Do:** cap the row's inner grid ~1100px, keep the row background and `border-b` full-bleed so the log still reads edge-to-edge; move `+ Add entry` beside the totals.

### `/reports` — **the strongest case for width, the worst execution of it.**

- **Measured**: the search input is **1108px wide** at 1600 (`filter-bar.tsx:103`, `flex min-w-[12rem] flex-1`). A 1108px single-line text box for typing "invoice" is the most conspicuously wrong element in the app and the thing that makes the page read as generated.
- Filter row 1 (arrows + Day/Week/Month + date range) uses ~700px and leaves ~1150px empty; row 3 (three preset chips) uses ~280px. Two of three filter rows are >60% void while the middle one is 80% one input.
- The results summary (`:139`) is unconstrained prose.
- The log inherits every `/timer` row problem, plus the loading-empty-state bug.

**Do:** cap the search at `max-w-[24rem]`, drop `flex-1`. Then decide: either **spend** the width (project name always visible, `TagChips` — already written at `project-dot.tsx:59`, never rendered — and the note as a real second column rather than a truncated sub-line), or cap the log like `/timer`. Right now it is claimed and unspent.

### `/projects` — **clearly wrong. The worst page of the four.** *(measured)*

`projects.tsx:42` renders `<ul className="… rounded-md border">` full-bleed. Measured child boxes of a row at 1600: swatch `x=293 w=16` → name button `x=317 **w=1050**` → "Billable by default" `x=1375 w=118` → actions `x=1505 w=58`. Row width 1294, **left-to-right gap 1196px**. The name button is 1050px wide to hold the string "V3", and the two action icons are `sm:opacity-0` until hover (`:409`) — so at rest, a 1294×40px bordered box contains a dot, one word, and a checkbox 1200px away. This is verbatim the kilometre-wide-row failure. The `<h1>Projects</h1>` + `New project` header pair (`:32-34`) has the same problem at 1114px.

**Do:** `max-w-[46rem]` on the section container at `projects.tsx:30`. This is a settings-shaped list, not a table.

### `/settings` — **clearly wrong, and half-fixed already. Cheapest fix in the review.** *(measured)*

The hints correctly self-constrain — measured **534px = exactly 65ch** desktop, 41.8ch mobile — which proves the `app-shell.tsx:69-84` local-constraint strategy works. And the inner rows are fine: measured gaps of 66–138px ("24-hour → 17:30" is 69px apart). So this page is *not* suffering the row problem.

What it is suffering is the **containers**. `Section` at `settings.tsx:217` carries `border-b border-edge-soft` at full width, so a **1329px hairline runs underneath a 272px `<select>`**, six times down the page. Correctly-measured 65ch paragraphs float in the left third while every divider is three to five times longer than the widest thing it separates. That mismatch is exactly what "unanchored" looks like. Compounding it: `<h1>Settings</h1>` at `:49` is `text-sm font-semibold` — *smaller* than a day total on `/timer` (`text-base font-semibold`), so the page has no anchor at either end.

**Do:** `max-w-[46rem]` on the column at `settings.tsx:48`. One line. Highest ratio of improvement to effort in this entire review.

### Summary

**Full width serves zero of the four pages as currently implemented.** It is *defensible* for `/timer` and `/reports` if the row grids get an inner cap and Reports actually spends the width on columns. It is simply wrong for `/projects` and `/settings`, where one line each fixes it. Do not revert `app-shell.tsx:85` — the log genuinely wants to be edge-to-edge. Constrain the content.

## Questions to Consider

1. **What is the widest useful width for a row here — and what goes in the extra space when you find it?** The commit's argument ("the width goes to the title and the note") is right in principle and false in implementation: `flex-1` on the title *container* spends the width on void, not on text. If the answer is "the note becomes a real second column," full width is earned. If the answer is "nothing," the cap belongs at ~1100px and the debate is over.
2. **Why does moving an entry to another day owe the user a sentence, but changing its billable duration doesn't?** You already have the principle, the toast, and the undo pattern. The duration edit is the one that ends up on the invoice.
3. **The Hatch Rule is implemented for missing notes and not for gaps in the day.** "Where did the day go" is the question the product is named after. What would a 2h 40m hatched band between 11:20 and 14:05 do to how this feels at 6pm?
4. **The note sheet's doc comment says three cheap ways out. Two of them destroy the note.** Is "cheap to leave" still the right principle if leaving is lossy, or is the actual principle "cheap to leave *and impossible to lose*"?
5. **The sidebar is stock base-luma inside a hand-tuned product.** It is the one element visible on 100% of screens 100% of the time. What does it cost to bring `rounded-xl` down to `rounded-md` and give the active item a real boundary instead of a 0.04-lightness fill step?
6. **Enter starts the timer only from the title field.** For a tool whose principle is "keyboard before pointer" and whose context is a pinned tab, what is the global binding — and why isn't it in the `?` overlay yet?
